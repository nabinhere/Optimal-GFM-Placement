# Replication of System-Strength-Constrained Grid-Forming Energy Storage Planning in Renewable Power Systems

*Author Names, NC State University*

***Abstract*—Grid-following (GFL) inverter-based resources can lose small-signal stability in weak power grids, and grid-forming (GFM) energy storage systems (ESSs) are increasingly deployed to strengthen them. This paper presents an independent Python replication of a recently proposed planning method that sizes and sites GFM ESSs at minimum cost subject to a generalized short-circuit ratio (gSCR) constraint. We implement the network model, the gSCR and its analytic sensitivities, the support-function-based iterative algorithm for unrestricted siting, and the sensitivity-ranked two-layer method for a limited number of sites. We further show that the unrestricted problem is equivalent to a convex semidefinite program, which we use to certify global optimality, and verify the two-layer method by exhaustive enumeration. On the modified IEEE 39-bus system, the replication reproduces the reported baseline gSCR, sensitivity rankings, optimal costs, and optimal sites, including cost reductions of up to 41% relative to a centralized benchmark. Time-domain and modal simulations with a purpose-designed GFL controller confirm that the planned system is stable when the gSCR exceeds its critical value of 2.5. On the IEEE 118-bus system, the results deviate from those reported; we show that several reported costs fall below a certified lower bound under the standard network data, indicating undisclosed data modifications.**

***Index Terms*—Grid-forming energy storage, generalized short-circuit ratio, system strength, optimal planning, semidefinite programming, replication study.**
